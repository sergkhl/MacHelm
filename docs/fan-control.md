# Fan Control

Fan Control is an opt-in temperature policy with a signed root helper. The app presents the profile, state, and recovery actions; the helper owns fan commands and verifies their effects. This document describes the 0.1 source behavior and the selected local checks below. It does not establish general hardware or distribution qualification.

## Behavior

The default ramp starts at **70 °C** and reaches maximum demand at **85 °C**. The firmware CPU die average (`TCMb`) drives both the start threshold and the ramp with five-second elapsed-time smoothing. Each fan receives the same normalized demand through its own RPM range; speed decreases are limited to 300 RPM per second.

Normal custom control ends once the smoothed average has stayed below the start temperature for ten seconds of usable readings. Meanwhile the demand falls toward the fans' minimum under the same limit. The count restarts after a reading at or above the start, an unusable frame, or a gap of three seconds, and it also runs during protection. There is no temperature margin below the start. A 5 °C margin was removed on 2026-10-13. The ten-second count was added on 2026-10-19, after a one-reading dip released control and took it back within two seconds ([ADR 0009](adr/0009-release-by-mode-and-recover-an-unpredicted-entry.md)).

Enabling the policy below the start temperature observes the fans without issuing fan writes. Crossing the threshold acquires manual control. Staying below it for ten seconds releases an existing override once, while the enabled policy continues observing. It does not repeatedly reset fans that Apple already controls. Apple automatic does not mean stopped fans: [Apple describes temperature-driven cooling](https://support.apple.com/en-us/101576) for multiple internal components. MacHelm does not force zero RPM below the threshold. The separate protection rules below can require cooling even when the CPU average is low.

Protection requests maximum demand when the CPU die maximum (`TCMz`) stays at or above 90 °C for three usable readings spanning at least 1.9 seconds, a monitored GPU temperature reaches 85 °C, or macOS reports serious or critical thermal pressure. Protection remains active until ten seconds of cool CPU/GPU readings and nominal or fair pressure. A missing or invalid frame never supplies a replacement temperature for control.

The Cooling page shows the current state, readings, ramp settings, and helper controls. The Overview exposes the main switch and any recovery requirement. The optional menu bar readout shows CPU temperature and the faster fan's speed while fresh readings are available.

A recovery hold that waits on the owner raises a macOS notification. It also puts a warning sign on the menu bar item, whether or not the readout is shown. A hold the helper clears by itself raises a notification too, because Fan Control then stays paused. A restoration the helper cannot complete while it owns an override is not a hold, but a fan may be left in manual mode. The warning sign shows for it too, and a notification follows once it has lasted ten seconds. Each notification is announced once, also across launches, and withdrawn once what it describes has ended. MacHelm asks for notification permission when Fan Control is turned on. Its Info.plist asks for alerts that stay on screen until dismissed, but macOS decides. On the owner's Mac (macOS 26.6.2) MacHelm got the Temporary style without any change by the owner, so its notifications appear as banners that go away by themselves. Choosing Persistent in System Settings → Notifications → MacHelm keeps them on screen.

The enabled setting is remembered. At launch or after wake, resumption requires three healthy readings, automatic fan state, no retained override or recovery hold, and no detected competing utility. Turning Fan Control off or quitting requests release; the helper also handles sleep, a lost client connection, and expiration of the client's five-second lease.

## Ownership and recovery

One authenticated XPC connection owns a control session. Another client cannot renew, update, or stop it. The interface accepts validated profiles and session/recovery identifiers; it does not accept arbitrary SMC keys, commands, sensor values, or target speeds. App and helper must agree on protocol version 4. Protocol and recovery-record versions are independent of the app's 0.1 version.

Before each hardware write, the helper records durable intent under `/Library/Application Support/io.github.sergkhl.MacHelm.FanControlHelper/`. A command is acknowledged only after the required native readbacks agree. An uncertain write remains a recovery requirement rather than being treated as a successful release.

Manual entry allows the firmware-selected target to differ from the last automatic target for the fan being acquired: any target within that fan's range, or zero. Two distinct probes must confirm a stable manual target before it becomes the owned baseline. Other owned fans and the global unlock must still match. Once acquired, ordinary target ownership stays strict. On **2026-10-19** a manual entry landed at 0 RPM on Mac14,9. It came 0.6 s after the zero target written at that fan's release, and 43 ms after the release was confirmed. A development build 7 helper held it as changed ownership.

A release sets each fan in manual mode, and the fan named by an unconfirmed command, back to automatic mode, and writes nothing else. Until development build 11 a zero target followed for each fan. Firmware can keep such a target while the fan reads automatic and apply it at the next manual entry, so it is no longer written.

A mode release has the matching allowance. Firmware can apply its own automatic target to the fan being released before that fan's mode reads automatic. While it still reads manual, that fan alone may show a target within its RPM range; other owned fans and the global unlock must still match, and only automatic mode in two probes confirms the release. A release that never reads automatic stays unconfirmed at its deadline, and the helper's own recovery attempt accepts the same state at its start. On **2026-10-14** a development build 4 release on Mac14,9 without this allowance was held as changed ownership, although both fans then read automatic.

During acknowledgement, the helper exclusively renames the already-durable pending record into its conservative crash marker, then saves the acknowledged record. Two distinct complete native readbacks must agree within one second of the hardware write returning. Once those probes agree, durable journal publication has a separate two-second allowance. This prevents a confirmed command from timing out merely because a filesystem sync extends beyond the hardware-confirmation window. Publication still checks the clock, ownership and competing utility; manual entry also commits through the live session's safety gate. Failed or late publication leaves the original pending intent available on restart; only successful publication permits marker retirement. Diagnostics record hardware confirmation and journal publication times separately.

Where its ownership and journal checks permit, the helper can make one bounded attempt to return its own fans to automatic after an unconfirmed operation. A manual entry that landed at a target the record cannot predict keeps that attempt while no known fan utility runs. That fan reads manual at any target, and every other fan and the unlock read as recorded. Any other change in fan state, a known competing utility, a clock fault or a journal failure declines it. Development build 7 declined the 0 RPM entry of 2026-10-19, which left fan 0 stopped in manual mode for about five hours. An unresolved hold keeps activation and helper removal blocked. The Cooling page offers **Restore automatic now** and **Verify external recovery**. The latter checks automatic state after recovery performed outside MacHelm and issues no fan writes. Records from another boot or helper build require the supported external-verification path.

If persistence for an unconfirmed fan operation or its recovery fails specifically with `ENOSPC` (no space left on device), the helper keeps the hold and checks actual durable journal writability again at most once every 15 seconds. It resumes a release that was blocked before its first recovery fan write. If release already ran, recovery retries only fresh automatic-state verification and journal retirement, with no further fan writes. A durable phase records the no-repeat boundary before recovery can write fans, so a same-boot, same-build helper restart can resume a not-yet-issued release or verify retirement read-only. A restart after that boundary cannot repeat release writes; fans still reading manual require the owner's recovery action. Other storage errors, changed ownership, a known competing utility, another boot/build, and malformed records retain the ordinary recovery hold. Recovery pauses for sleep and respects exclusive recovery requests. Successful recovery leaves Fan Control paused until explicitly enabled again.

The conflict check recognizes Macs Fan Control. It reads a process's path only when the kernel's short name for that process can be the utility's executable name; [ADR 0006](adr/0006-processor-time-only-for-what-is-watched.md) gives the measurements behind this. It cannot identify every possible writer. A changed fan mode or target causes the controller to stop its ordinary control path rather than repeatedly overwrite another controller's state.

## Build and signing

Use the MacHelm scheme in `MacHelm.xcodeproj`. The app and `FanControlHelper` must be signed by the same real Apple team. The other embedded targets should use that team too. An ad-hoc signature does not satisfy helper authentication.

```sh
MACHELM_DEVELOPMENT_TEAM=YOURTEAMID \
  ./scripts/build-fan-artifact.sh Debug
```

Set `MACHELM_SIGN_IDENTITY` if a specific installed signing identity is needed. The script builds the complete app and runs the fan and toolkit artifact checks. It does not register a helper, approve a background item, or activate Fan Control.

After placing a signed app in `/Applications/MacHelm.app`, use its helper setup control and complete macOS's background-item approval. Check the state shown by the app before enabling Fan Control. The fresh MacHelm namespace does not adopt helpers or recovery records from earlier private installations.

For a local Release build, use the same build command with `Release` in place of `Debug`, or select the Release configuration in Xcode. Developer ID signing, Apple notarization, and binary publication are separate distribution steps. They are not established by the 0.1 source release.

## Verification and limits

- **Source tests:** `swift test --scratch-path build/swift-tests` exercises policies, lease ownership, recovery, native-adapter behavior over fake transports, and acceptance-client logic. These tests do not command physical fans.
- **Artifact checks:** `scripts/verify-fan-artifact.sh <app>` and `scripts/verify-toolkit-artifact.sh <app>` inspect bundle structure, identity, signatures, and embedded components. They do not prove that an installed helper is approved or reachable.
- **Native checks:** the [acceptance client](../Sources/FanControlAcceptance/README.md) can observe an authenticated installed helper or run a separately selected bounded control trial. Native evidence belongs to the exact app/helper build, model, OS, and action that was exercised.

On **2026-10-13**, the signed universal **Release version 0.1, development build 2** was installed and checked on **Mac14,9 (M2 Pro, two fans)** running **macOS 26.6.2 (25G83)**. The final Swift suite passed **681 tests with zero failures**; the Xcode project generator check and both signed-artifact checks passed. Installed fan-helper approval, authenticated reachability, and the running executable's installed-bundle path were checked separately from the artifact inspections.

Selected native GUI checks covered two 30-sample protection windows, verified release to effective Apple automatic, and 30 fresh below-threshold samples with the policy still enabled. During the latter window there was no owned override or recovery hold, and the last native-write timestamp stayed unchanged. Restoring the saved **65/85 °C** profile produced an enabled session that entered ordinary control at the smoothed threshold. One normal quit/relaunch check verified automatic release on quit and remembered resumption; a further 12-sample observation included ordinary control, cooling release, and continued enabled automatic observation without a new recovery hold or acknowledgement deadline overrun.

A separate ordinary native acceptance trial used a temporary **45/85 °C** profile and a **30-second maximum bound**, completing successfully in **6.56 seconds**. Both native target registers matched the request within 2 RPM, and both measured fan speeds met the ±10% convergence criterion after two stable fresh frames. A second authenticated connection could not acquire, renew, update, or stop the first connection's lease. Final release verified both effective fan modes as automatic with no lease, owned override, or recovery hold. This was a short bounded trial, not a sustained 30-second run; it did not change the saved GUI profile.

The source has an explicit M2-family sensor map and rejects unsupported model identities or missing required telemetry. Inclusion in that map is not hardware qualification, and fanless models cannot provide controllable fans. These checks qualify only the selected actions on the exact local build, model, and OS above. Other models, broad fault/sleep/disconnect scenarios, sustained operation, Developer ID signing, notarization, and binary distribution remain unqualified for development build 2. Historical checks from earlier installations do not extend this scope.

Also on **2026-10-13**, signed universal **Release version 0.1, development build 3** was installed on that same Mac and OS with the acknowledgement-publication and storage-recovery changes. The full source suite passed **692 tests with zero failures**, including slow durable publication and injected out-of-space failures before release and after verified release. Those tests also cover retry throttling, same-boot restart boundaries, competing utilities, permission/I/O failures and sleep/wake. The project generator check and both signed-artifact checks passed. The approved, authenticated running helper was checked separately at `/Applications/MacHelm.app/Contents/Library/HelperTools/FanControlHelper`, with development build 3 and code-directory hash `40363bad674cca09a77fbeb5f8b0c8ae20d3a808`.

Development build 3's bounded native **45/85 °C** round trip completed in **8.57 seconds** within its selected 30-second limit. It checked effective target readbacks, measured fan response, rejection of a second connection's lease actions, and final verified automatic release with no ownership or recovery hold. Captured manual-entry, target and release diagnostics reported neither hardware-confirmation nor publication overruns. After restoring the enabled saved **65/85 °C** profile, **15 distinct fresh frames spanning 28.59 seconds** covered ordinary control, below-threshold release and reacquisition, with no recovery hold or captured acknowledgement overrun. Two preceding strict one-second observation runs stopped on repeated cached complete frames; they are not successful fresh-frame qualifications. The spaced observation is separate evidence and does not erase those results. Native checks did not fill the disk or inject storage failures: automatic recovery from `ENOSPC` is established by source fault-injection tests, not a physical full-disk trial. Keep Awake remained enabled after the supported helper update.

Later on **2026-10-13**, signed universal **Release version 0.1, development build 4** was installed on that same Mac and OS with the narrowed competing-utility check and the app changes of [ADR 0006](adr/0006-processor-time-only-for-what-is-watched.md). Development build 4's check collected twelve authenticated status frames. Its bounded native **45/85 °C** round trip completed in **3.98 seconds** within its selected 30-second limit: both measured fan speeds converged, a second connection's lease actions were rejected, and release was verified as effective automatic. Sleep, disconnect, fault and sustained scenarios were not run again on development build 4.

On **2026-10-19**, signed universal **Release version 0.1, development build 11** was installed on that same Mac and OS with the changes of [ADR 0009](adr/0009-release-by-mode-and-recover-an-unpredicted-entry.md). The full source suite passed **730 tests with zero failures**, and the project generator check and both signed-artifact checks passed. Both helpers were removed and set up again from the Cooling page, and macOS asked for no new approval. The running fan helper was then checked at `/Applications/MacHelm.app/Contents/Library/HelperTools/FanControlHelper`, with development build 11 and code-directory hash `ccf5bee3488fb20b2c4a0f369706f8ba6658de66`.

Development build 11's bounded native **45/85 °C** round trip completed in **4.25 seconds** within its selected 30-second limit.

- **Control.** Both measured fan speeds converged, within 0.7% and 1.1% of the request.
- **Second connection.** Its lease actions were rejected.
- **Release.** Verified as effective automatic. It wrote automatic mode for each fan and no zero target: two bounded writes in 0.50 s, the last acknowledged 238 ms after its native return.
- **Target registers after release.** Right after release they still read the session's last manual targets, 2858 and 2889 RPM. Twenty seconds later they read the firmware's own 2317 and 2502 RPM.

The zero entry target, the recovery attempt for an unpredicted entry, the ten-second release wait and the alerts were not exercised on hardware; source tests cover them.

Later on **2026-10-19**, development build 11 was checked further on that same Mac and OS. In ordinary use its log showed the ten-second wait: control ended 10.0 s after the first smoothed reading below the 70 °C start, with every reading in between below it, and the release wrote automatic mode only. A qualification build of the same source, compiled with `FAN_CONTROL_QUALIFICATION`, was then installed for one injected case. The helper wrote fan 0's first target, 4,851 RPM, and was told to treat its acknowledgement as unconfirmed.

- **The helper's own attempt.** It wrote fan 0's automatic mode and nothing else, 18 ms after the command's deadline. Its settle check found both fans automatic, and Fan Control was left paused about 2.7 s after the begin. Fan 1 was never written.
- **The notification.** MacHelm posted "Fan Control paused", and the owner saw the banner. MacHelm's notifications were allowed, with the alert style Temporary although the owner had never changed it, so the banner went away by itself. MacHelm withdrew it once Fan Control was turned on again.
- **Not produced.** A hold that waits on the owner, with its notification and warning sign: the injection only creates outcomes the helper may recover from by itself. Restore automatic now, the zero entry target and the attempt after an unpredicted entry also remain unseen on hardware. Every entry in these checks started from spinning fans and landed in range.

The production app of development build 11 was put back afterwards, and Fan Control turned on again.

AppleSMC and the thermal interfaces used here are undocumented and may change with macOS. Already-issued synchronous kernel or filesystem calls cannot be preempted. The helper's deadlines restrict admission and confirmation of operations; they do not guarantee a hard wall-clock restoration deadline. A successful build, source test, or signature inspection cannot substitute for native release and recovery checks.
